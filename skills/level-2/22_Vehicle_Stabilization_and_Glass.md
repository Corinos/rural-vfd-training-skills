# DRILL 22: Vehicle Stabilization & Glass Management
**Category:** Vehicle Extrication | **Standard:** NFPA 1001 Level 2 (JPR 5.4.1) / Rural Rescue  
**Est. Time:** 45–60 min | **Apparatus:** Rescue / Engine | **PPE:** Extrication Gloves, Bunker Gear, Eye & Respiratory Protection  
**Roles:** Drill Leader (Facilitator) | Safety Officer (Watcher) | Stabilization & Glass Crews

---

### Objective & Rural Fireground Value
Every firefighter must be able to stabilize a vehicle on its wheels or side using **Step Chocks**, **Box Cribbing**, and **Rescue Struts**, chock the wheels to prevent roll, and safely manage **Tempered vs Laminated Glass** without spraying glass fragments over trapped patients. Unstable vehicles and uncontrolled glass cause severe responder and patient injuries at rural highway collisions.

---

### Equipment & Station Setup
- Training vehicle (scrap car, municipal pickup, or mock frame).
- 4 step chocks, 12–16 pieces of 4x4 box cribbing, 4 wedges.
- Mechanical or ratchet rescue struts (if equipped).
- Glass tools: Spring-loaded center punch, glass master / reciprocating saw, heavy duct tape or contact paper, canvas tarp / patient blanket.

---

### Safety Officer "Watch Outs" & The STOP Rule
- **Vehicle Suspension Movement:** Never place hands, arms, or boots underneath a vehicle's rocker panels or tires until stabilization is completed.
- **Glass Dust Inhalation:** Modern windshields (laminated glass) produce fine, hazardous glass dust when cut. Rescuers and patients must wear N95/particulate masks and safety glasses.
- **The STOP Rule:** Shout **"STOP!"** immediately if the vehicle rocks, shifts, or slips from cribbing while personnel are working around it.

---

### Step-by-Step Drill Progression

#### Phase 1: 4-Point Vehicle Stabilization (Wheels On Ground) (15 Minutes)
1. **Prevent Roll:** Place rubber or wooden wheel chocks tightly in front of and behind the uninjured rear tire.
2. **Defeat the Suspension (Step Chocking):**
   * A car resting solely on its inflated tires and coil springs will bounce violently during extrication tool use.
   * Slide **Step Chocks** beneath the rocker panels at four structural points:
     * Just behind the front wheels (front A-pillar).
     * Just ahead of the rear wheels (rear C-pillar).
   * Insert chocks with the steps facing upward; tap wooden wedges into any gaps until the car frame sits solid on the wood.
   * Deflate tires (pull valve stems) to settle the chassis firmly onto the step chocks.
   * Rock test: Push firmly on the vehicle fender. The vehicle should feel rigid with **zero suspension bounce**.

#### Phase 2: Box Cribbing (Vehicle On Side / Lifted Load) (15 Minutes)
1. **The 4x4 Box Crib:**
   * Build a stable box using two parallel 4x4 timbers, overlaid perpendicularly with two more 4x4s.
   * Rule of thumb: Crib height must never exceed **3 times the contact width** (e.g., an 18" wide base can only be stacked up to 54" high).
   * Top off with angled wedges to ensure 100% surface contact with the angled frame.

#### Phase 3: Glass Management (Tempered vs Laminated) (20 Minutes)
1. **Identify the Glass:**
   * **Tempered Glass (Side & Rear Windows):** Shatters into thousands of small, blunt cubes when struck.
   * **Laminated Glass (Windshields & Many Modern Side Windows):** Two layers of glass bonded to a plastic sheet; will not shatter with a center punch. Look for the "Laminated" bug/stamp in the corner!
2. **Managing Tempered Glass (Side Windows):**
   * Cover window with adhesive film or cross-strips of heavy duct tape to capture fragments.
   * Cover interior patient with a heavy blanket or salvage tarp.
   * Position a **Spring-Loaded Center Punch** in the **lowest, outermost corner** of the window (never center of glass!).
   * Push until punch clicks. Window shatters into tape. Rake glass fragments outward using a gloved hand or tool.
3. **Managing Laminated Glass (Windshield):**
   * Cut around the perimeter using a manual **Glass Master** or reciprocating saw (fine metal blade, 14–18 TPI).
   * Spray water or shaving cream along the cut line to capture glass dust.
   * Peel the cut windshield outward and discard under the vehicle or in the scrap pile.

---

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid
- *Punching tempered glass in the center:* The center of the glass flexes and resists the punch. Always punch the lower corner where glass is stiffest!
- *Stacking cribbing too high:* Tapering or building a narrow, wobbly tower will kick out when hydraulic spreaders exert 10,000+ lbs of force on the door!
- *Ignoring patient protection:* Shattering a side window without shielding the driver's face sprays sharp cubes directly into their eyes.

### Tailgate Debrief & Truck Reset Checklist
1. **Tailgate Questions:** *"Did the vehicle bounce after chocks were set?"*, *"How did we distinguish between tempered and laminated side glass?"*
2. **Reset Checklist:**
   - [ ] Glass shards swept clear of training area.
   - [ ] Cribbing and step chocks inspected for splits or rot; stacked neatly in truck trays.
   - [ ] Center punches and saw blades cleaned and returned to rescue box.
   - [ ] Attendance roster signed.
