# DRILL 24: Vehicle Battery Isolation – 12V Systems & Electric Vehicles (EV)
**Category:** Vehicle Extrication / Hazmat | **Standard:** NFPA 1001 Level 2 (JPR 5.4.1) / NFPA 1072  
**Est. Time:** 45–60 min | **Apparatus:** Rescue / Engine | **PPE:** Bunker Gear, Safety Glasses & Work/Extrication Gloves  
**Roles:** Drill Leader (Facilitator) | Safety Officer (Watcher) | Battery Isolation Team

---

### Objective & Rural Fireground Value
Every firefighter must know how to safely locate and isolate conventional **12-Volt vehicle batteries** to disarm airbag and electrical fire hazards, and recognize modern **Electric Vehicle (EV) and Hybrid** safety protocols: locating emergency cut loops/first responder disconnects, identifying **bright orange high-voltage cabling**, and understanding high-voltage thermal runaway hazards.

---

### Equipment & Station Setup
- Training vehicle or modern vehicle (volunteer or municipal truck).
- Hand tool battery isolation kit: 10mm/1/2" ratchet wrench, wire cutters/cable shears, electrical tape.
- Emergency Response Guide (ERG) or modern vehicle extrication field guide app (e.g., Euro NCAP / NFPA EV field guide).
- Thermal Imaging Camera (TIC).

---

### Safety Officer "Watch Outs" & The STOP Rule
- **THE HIGH-VOLTAGE CARDINAL RULE:** **NEVER CUT, PINCH, OR TOUCH BRIGHT ORANGE CABLES!** High-voltage cables in EVs carry 400 to 800+ Volts DC—instantly fatal.
- **Airbag Capacitor Bleed Time:** Disconnecting the 12V battery does **not** instantly disarm airbags! Capacitors store backup power for **up to 5 to 10 minutes** after battery isolation. Treat all undeployed airbags as loaded weapons.
- **The STOP Rule:** Shout **"STOP!"** immediately if anyone points a cutting tool near orange cables, or if an EV battery pack shows signs of off-gassing, hissing, or thermal runaway.

### Step-by-Step Drill Progression

#### Phase 1: Conventional 12-Volt Battery Isolation (15 Minutes)
1. **Why Isolate 12V First?**
   * Eliminates electrical ignition sources around leaking gasoline/antifreeze.
   * Cuts power to undeployed Supplemental Restraint Systems (SRS airbags, seatbelt pretensioners).
   * Shuts down high-voltage contactors in electric vehicles (EVs require 12V to energize high-voltage relays!).
2. **The Disconnect Sequence:**
   * Pop hood or trunk (look for auxiliary battery locations in trunk or under rear seats).
   * Identify terminals: **Negative (-)** (usually black or bare chassis ground) and **Positive (+)** (usually red or plastic cover).
   * **Rule: ALWAYS DISCONNECT NEGATIVE (-) FIRST!**
     * If your wrench touches the metal car chassis while loosening the negative terminal, nothing happens.
     * If you touch the positive terminal first and hit the chassis with your metal wrench, you cause a violent high-amp short circuit, sparks, and battery explosion!
   * If cutting cables: Make **two cuts** removing a 2-inch (5cm) chunk of the negative cable so the springy wire ends cannot accidentally spring back and re-contact!

#### Phase 2: Electric Vehicle (EV) Identification & High-Voltage Systems (15 Minutes)
1. **Identify the EV/Hybrid:**
   * Badges (Hybrid, EV, PHEV, BEV, Lightning, Tesla, Mach-E).
   * Lack of tailpipes / exhaust system.
   * Exterior charging port doors.
   * Large high-voltage battery casing bolted beneath the floor pan.
2. **Visualizing the Orange Cables:**
   * Locate the inverter, electric drive motors, and high-voltage conduit.
   * Reinforce: All 400V–800V cables are coded in **BRIGHT ORANGE**. They are completely off-limits to extrication tools.

#### Phase 3: EV First Responder Cut Loops & Service Plugs (20 Minutes)
1. **The Emergency Responder Cut Loop:**
   * Most modern EVs feature a low-voltage **First Responder Cut Loop** or emergency disconnect plug (commonly located under the front hood, near the fuse box, or behind the rear trunk trim).
   * Look for the bright colored tag with a scissors icon or pull-loop.
   * Cutting this low-voltage loop immediately opens the internal high-voltage contactors, trapping high voltage securely inside the sealed battery casing.
2. **EV Fire & Submersion Precautions:**
   * If an EV battery is damaged or burning, water requirements are massive (minimum **10,000 to 20,000 Litres** / 3,000–5,000 gallons) to cool the battery cell casing.
   * Use a TIC to monitor battery casing temperature continuously from a safe distance.

---

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid
- *Cutting positive battery terminal first:* A metal tool bridging the positive post to the fender creates a blinding arc flash and molten metal spatter. Always isolate the negative ground first!
- *Cutting into B-pillars without checking for seatbelt inflators:* Modern B-pillars contain pyrotechnic gas cylinders. Cutting through an inflator causes an explosive metal rupture. Peel plastic trim back to inspect before cutting!
- *Assuming an EV is off because the engine is silent:* An EV can be fully energized and in gear without making any sound. Always chock wheels and remove the key fob 50+ feet away from the vehicle.
