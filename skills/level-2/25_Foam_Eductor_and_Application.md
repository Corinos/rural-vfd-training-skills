# DRILL 25: Foam Eductors & Firefighting Foam Application
**Category:** Advanced Fire Suppression | **Standard:** NFPA 1001 Level 2 (JPR 5.3.1) / NFPA 11  
**Est. Time:** 45–60 min | **Apparatus:** Pumper / Engine | **PPE:** Full Structural Turnout Gear & Gloves  
**Roles:** Drill Leader (Facilitator) | Safety Officer (Watcher) | Nozzle Team & Pump Operator

---

### Objective & Rural Fireground Value
Every firefighter must know how to properly rig an **In-Line Foam Eductor** to an attack line, set appropriate metering percentages for **Class A (wildland/structure)** and **Class B (flammable liquids)** foam concentrates, understand backpressure limitations, and apply foam using the three classic techniques: **Roll-On, Bank-Down**, and **Rain-Down**.

---

### Equipment & Station Setup
- Apparatus pumper with 1.75" (45mm) discharge.
- 95 GPM (or 125 GPM) in-line foam eductor with metering dial and pick-up tube.
- 150 to 200 feet of 1.75" hose line (maximum allowable length downstream of eductor).
- Matching 95 GPM (or 125 GPM) foam or combination fog nozzle.
- 5-gallon pail of training foam concentrate (or water with dish soap for zero-cost environmental drill).

---

### Safety Officer "Watch Outs" & The STOP Rule
- **Foam Concentrate Exposure:** Foam concentrate is irritating to skin and eyes. Wear eye protection and structural gloves while handling concentrate pails and pickup tubes.
- **Environmental Runoff:** Ensure foam solutions do not drain directly into local salmon rivers, brooks, or storm sewers. Conduct drill over contained asphalt or gravel.
- **The STOP Rule:** Shout **"STOP!"** immediately if the nozzle stream collapses or if foam concentrate spills uncontrollably.

---

### Step-by-Step Drill Progression

#### Phase 1: The Physics of the In-Line Eductor (15 Minutes)
1. **The Venturi Effect:**
   * Water passing through a restricted orifice creates a low-pressure vacuum that draws foam concentrate up the pick-up tube.
2. **The 4 Rules of Foam Eductor Success (Why Eductors Fail):**
   * **Rule 1: Inlet Pressure:** The pump operator must deliver **200 PSI** to the inlet of the eductor. Any less will not create sufficient vacuum.
   * **Rule 2: Matching Gallonage:** The nozzle GPM **MUST MATCH** the eductor GPM rating! (e.g., A 95 GPM eductor requires a 95 GPM nozzle setting. If the nozzle is set to 60 or 150 GPM, foam will not draft!).
   * **Rule 3: Maximum Hose Length:** Never exceed **200 feet (60m)** of 1.75" hose between the eductor and the nozzle. Excessive downstream friction causes backpressure that kills the Venturi vacuum.
   * **Rule 4: Elevation Limit:** Keep the nozzle within 10 to 15 feet of elevation relative to the eductor.

#### Phase 2: Metering Dial Settings (10 Minutes)
* **Class A Foam (Wood, Paper, Structural, Brush):**
  * Set dial to **0.3% to 0.5%** for initial structural knockdown.
  * Set dial to **0.1% to 0.2%** for deep penetration during overhaul.
* **Class B Hydrocarbons (Gasoline, Diesel, Heating Oil):**
  * Set dial to **1% or 3%** (check manufacturer pail instructions).
* **Class B Polar Solvents (Ethanol, E85, Alcohol, Brake Fluid):**
  * Requires AR-AFFF (Alcohol Resistant foam); set dial to **3% or 6%**.

#### Phase 3: The 3 Application Techniques (25 Minutes)
1. **Technique 1: The Roll-On Method:**
   * Used on pool/spill fires on flat ground.
   * Direct the foam stream at the ground **just in front of the burning spill**.
   * The foam blanket builds up and rolls forward across the liquid surface, smothering vapors without agitating the fuel.
2. **Technique 2: The Bank-Down Method:**
   * Used when an obstacle or vertical wall is present (e.g., burning tanker trailer, wall, engine compartment).
   * Direct the stream against the vertical wall just above the liquid.
   * Foam bounces off the surface and runs gently down to cover the fuel pool.
3. **Technique 3: The Rain-Down Method:**
   * Used when roll-on or bank-down are obstructed.
   * Direct stream high into the air at a 60-degree angle over the fire.
   * Allow the foam to break apart and fall gently down like snowflakes over the spill surface (never plunge stream directly into fuel!).

---

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid
- *Plunging the stream directly into the fuel:* Shooting a high-velocity stream into burning liquid splashes burning fuel across the ground and breaks up the foam blanket!
- *Partially closing the nozzle bail:* Gating or partially throttling the nozzle increases downstream backpressure, instantly halting foam induction at the eductor.
- *Kinked hose downstream:* Any kink creates backpressure that causes the eductor to stop picking up concentrate.

### Tailgate Debrief & Truck Reset Checklist
1. **Tailgate Questions:** *"Why did the eductor stop pulling foam when the nozzle bail was half closed?"*, *"Which application technique produces the gentlest foam blanket?"*
2. **Reset Checklist:**
   - [ ] Flush the eductor: Submerge pick-up tube in clean bucket of water and flow line for 60 seconds to wash corrosive foam out of the ball check valve.
   - [ ] Hose drained, washed, and repacked.
   - [ ] Concentrate pails sealed tightly and stowed upright in compartment.
   - [ ] Attendance roster signed.
