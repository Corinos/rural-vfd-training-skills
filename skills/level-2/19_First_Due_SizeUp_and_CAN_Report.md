# DRILL 19: First-Due Size-Up & The CAN Radio Report
**Category:** Incident Command & Communication | **Standard:** NFPA 1001 Level 2 (JPR 5.1.1, 5.1.2) / WorkSafeNB Reg 91-191  
**Est. Time:** 45–60 min | **Apparatus:** Any Engine/Command | **PPE:** Structural Turnout Gear or Duty Uniform + Portable Radio  
**Roles:** Drill Leader (Facilitator) | Safety Officer (Watcher) | Senior Firefighter / Acting Officer Participants

---

### Objective & Rural Fireground Value
Every firefighter (especially senior firefighters stepping up when officers are absent) must be able to conduct a rapid **360-degree size-up** upon arrival, transmit a professional **Initial Radio Report (IRR)**, enforce **WorkSafeNB 2-In / 2-Out** rules, and deliver structured progress reports using the **CAN (Conditions, Actions, Needs)** format.

---

### Equipment & Station Setup
- Portable handheld radios on station tactical channel (e.g., Tac 1).
- Training scenario flashcards or photos of local rural residential/commercial structures projected on a screen or clipboard.
- Notepad / grease-pencil command board.

---

### Safety Officer "Watch Outs" & The STOP Rule
- **WorkSafeNB Minimum Staffing Law:** Remind all participants that under WorkSafeNB Reg 91-191 Section 51.6(2) and NFPA 1500, a minimum of **4 firefighters on scene** is legally required before entering an IDLH structural fire (2 inside, 2 standby outside). Arriving with 2 or 3 firefighters means operating in **defensive or exterior transitional mode** unless an immediate, confirmed rescue exception applies!
- **Radio Discipline:** Enforce strict radio etiquette: think before you press the PTT (push-to-talk) button. No rambling.

---

### Step-by-Step Drill Progression

#### Phase 1: The 360-Degree Size-Up Walkaround (15 Minutes)
1. Arriving on scene, the first-due senior member conducts a **360-degree walkaround** (or visually checks all sides: Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, Delta).
2. **What to Look For:**
   * **Building:** Stories, construction (wood frame, brick, modular), roof type.
   * **Smoke & Fire:** Location, color, volume, velocity (where is it coming from?).
   * **Life Safety:** Occupancy status, cars in driveway, toys in yard, window signs.
   * **Basement / Grade Level:** Walkout basement on Side Charlie (common in NB hills!).
   * **Hazards:** Overhead power lines down, propane tanks, fuel oil tanks.

#### Phase 2: The Initial Radio Report (IRR) (15 Minutes)
Practice delivering the standardized 5-part radio report:
```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. Unit ID:          "Engine 1 on scene..."                                 |
| 2. Address/Location: "...at 4522 Route 105."                                |
| 3. Building Desc:    "We have a two-story wood-frame residential dwelling." |
| 4. Conditions:       "Working fire, heavy black smoke from Side Bravo 1st." |
| 5. Mode & Action:    "Engine 1 crew is laying a 1.75 crosslay for an        |
|                      exterior transitional attack. Establishing Main St     |
|                      Command. We are in defensive mode awaiting backup."    |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

#### Phase 3: The CAN Progress Report (20 Minutes)
When Incident Command or dispatch calls for an update, use the **CAN** format:
* **C – Conditions:** What do you see right now?  
  *(e.g., "Heavy smoke layer banking down to knee level; high heat in kitchen.")*
* **A – Actions:** What is your crew doing right now?  
  *(e.g., "Water is flowing on the ceiling; advancing toward the kitchen.")*
* **N – Needs:** What resources or help do you need next?  
  *(e.g., "Need horizontal ventilation at Side Bravo window; need Tanker 2 to dump water.")*

---

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid
- *Skipping the 360-degree walk:* A fire that looks small from the front (Side Alpha) may be fully engulfing a walkout basement at the rear (Side Charlie). Never commit crews without seeing the rear!
- *Entering IDLH with only 2 or 3 members on scene:* Violates provincial safety laws and puts members at extreme risk without a backup rescue team in place.
- *Rambling on the radio:* Keep transmissions under 10 seconds. Clear, crisp, and standardized.
