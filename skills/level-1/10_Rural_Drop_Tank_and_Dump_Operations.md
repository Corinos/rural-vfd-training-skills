# DRILL 10: Rural Drop Tank Deployment & Tanker Dump Operations
**Category:** Rural Water Supply | **Standard:** NFPA 1001 Level 1 (JPR 4.3.15) / FUS STSS Standard  
**Est. Time:** 60–75 min | **Apparatus:** Pumper & Tanker (Mobile Water Supply) | **PPE:** Full Gear or Work Gloves/Boots  
**Roles:** Drill Leader (Facilitator) | Safety Officer (Watcher) | Drop-Site Crew & Tanker Driver

---

### Objective & Rural Fireground Value
Every firefighter must be able to remove a 1,500 to 3,000-gallon portable folding drop tank (Porta-Tank) from apparatus racks, deploy it on level ground, stage a low-level strainer, set up a jet siphon between dual tanks, and guide a tanker into position to dump 1,500+ gallons in under 90 seconds. Mastering this drill is mandatory to pass and maintain **Fire Underwriters Survey (FUS) Superior Tanker Shuttle Service (STSS)** accreditation in New Brunswick.

---

### Equipment & Station Setup
- Pumper with hard suction hose (6-inch / 150mm) and low-level strainer with air-bleed float.
- Tanker with portable folding drop tank (2,000–3,000 gal) and 10-inch rear or swivel side dump valve.
- Jet siphon attachment and 1.75" discharge line.
- Ground tarp to protect drop tank liner from sharp gravel/debris.

---

### Safety Officer "Watch Outs" & The STOP Rule
- **Backing Apparatus Hazard:** The tanker must have a designated backing guide wearing a high-visibility vest. The guide must stay in the driver's left-side mirror at all times!
- **Pinch Points:** Keep hands clear of folding tank scissor frames when opening. Frame hinges can pinch or crush fingers.
- **Water Weight:** A 2,500-gallon tank weighs over 25,000 lbs (11,300 kg) when full! Once filled, it cannot be pushed or moved without draining.
- **The STOP Rule:** Shout **"STOP!"** immediately if the backing tanker loses sight of the guide or if the tank begins to collapse due to uneven ground slope.

---

### Step-by-Step Drill Progression

#### Phase 1: Site Selection & Tank Deployment (15 Minutes)
1. **Site Selection:**
   * Choose flat, firm ground adjacent to the attack pumper.
   * Ensure adequate lane clearance (minimum 12 feet) for incoming tankers to pull past, dump, and exit without turning around.
2. **Deploying the Folding Tank:**
   * Firefighters lay a heavy protective canvas/vinyl ground tarp on the road surface to prevent gravel punctures.
   * Release retaining straps and lift the folding tank off the tanker rack (2–4 firefighters).
   * Stand the folded tank vertically on its bottom rails.
   * Two firefighters grasp opposite corners of the frame, pull outward smoothly until frame locks open in a square/rectangle.
   * Push the internal vinyl/hypalon liner down into the corners; ensure the drain sleeve is cinched tight and tied to the top rail!

#### Phase 2: Low-Level Strainer & Hard Suction Staging (15 Minutes)
1. Connect low-level strainer to 10-foot section of 6-inch hard suction hose.
2. Lower strainer into the drop tank until it sits flat on the tank floor.
3. Secure the suction hose to the tank frame using a strap or short rope (prevents the strainer from flipping upside down when the pump pulls a vacuum).
4. Connect hard suction to the pumper's master intake.

#### Phase 3: Rapid Tanker Dump & Jet Siphon (25 Minutes)
1. **Guiding the Tanker to the Dump Site:**
   * Spotter directs the tanker driver using standard hand signals.
   * Position the tanker's 10-inch dump chute directly over the lip of the drop tank.
   * Tanker driver sets air brake and trips the air-actuated or manual dump valve.
   * Water dumps in a high-volume torrent (aim for 1,500–2,000 Imperial gallons offloaded in 60–90 seconds).
2. **Dual-Tank Jet Siphon Setup (For Continuous Flow):**
   * When setting up a second drop tank for high-flow shuttle operations, place Tank 2 end-to-end with Tank 1.
   * Place a **Jet Siphon** tube between Tank 2 (dump tank) and Tank 1 (drafting tank).
   * Connect a 1.75" hose line from the pumper discharge to the jet siphon inlet.
   * Flowing 50–75 PSI into the jet siphon creates a strong Venturi vacuum, transferring water continuously from Tank 2 into Tank 1 at over 500 GPM!

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid
- *Drain sleeve untied:* Dumping 2,000 gallons into a tank with the drain sleeve untied means half your water runs down the ditch!
- *Setting tank on steep road camber:* If the road crown is too steep, water will spill over the downhill rail before the tank is even 60% full.
- *Strainer vortexing:* At low water levels (under 6 inches), swirling air vortexes form. Keep a floating ball, plastic lid, or rubber mat over the strainer inlet to break up whirlpools.

---

### Tailgate Debrief & Truck Reset Checklist
1. **Tailgate Questions:** *"How many seconds did it take from tanker stopping to dump valve open?"*, *"Did the jet siphon keep Tank 1 level with Tank 2?"*
2. **Reset Checklist:**
   - [ ] Drain sleeve opened to release remaining water.
   - [ ] Liner wiped dry and swept clear of gravel/mud before folding.
   - [ ] Frame folded neatly and secured back in the apparatus mounting bracket.
   - [ ] Suction hose and strainer drained and mounted.
   - [ ] Tanker refilled 100% from station fill line.
   - [ ] Attendance roster signed.
