# DRILL 14: Conventional Forcible Entry ("The Irons")
**Category:** Forcible Entry | **Standard:** NFPA 1001 Level 1 (JPR 4.3.4) / WorkSafeNB Reg 91-191  
**Est. Time:** 45–60 min | **Apparatus:** Any Engine/Truck | **PPE:** Full Structural Turnout Gear, Eye Protection & Gloves  
**Roles:** Drill Leader (Facilitator) | Safety Officer (Watcher) | Halligan Firefighter & Axe/Striking Firefighter

---

### Objective & Rural Fireground Value
Every firefighter must be able to perform conventional forcible entry on residential and commercial doors using **"The Irons"** (Flathead Axe and Halligan Bar). The two-firefighter team must size up the door, determine swing direction, execute the 3-step sequence (**Gap, Set, Force**), and maintain door control. In rural areas, rapid entry without destroying unnecessary property saves minutes when seconds count.

---

### Equipment & Station Setup
- Forcible entry training door prop (or pallet/scrap 2x4 door simulator).
- 1 set of "The Irons" (8-lb or 6-lb flathead axe and 30-inch Halligan bar).
- Wooden door wedges / chocks.
- Eye protection (safety glasses mandatory under helmet visors).

---

### Safety Officer "Watch Outs" & The STOP Rule
- **Flying Debris & Glancing Blows:** The striking firefighter must swing the flathead axe with short, controlled, perpendicular strikes onto the Halligan strike plate. No wild baseball swings!
- **Hand Placement:** The Halligan firefighter must keep their hands wrapped around the shaft or fork—never curled over the strike plate or between the tool and door frame!
- **The STOP Rule:** Shout **"STOP!"** immediately if the axe head glances off the Halligan, if hands stray into the strike zone, or if the door pops unexpectedly.

---

### Step-by-Step Drill Progression

#### Phase 1: Size-Up & "Try Before You Pry" (10 Minutes)
1. **The Cardinal Rule:** **TRY BEFORE YOU PRY!** Rotate the knob and push/pull. You look foolish forcing an unlocked door!
2. **Door Construction & Swing Direction:**
   * **Hinges Visible:** Door swings **OUTWARD** toward you (common in commercial buildings, exterior storage rooms).
   * **No Hinges Visible (Frame Stop Visible):** Door swings **INWARD** away from you (standard residential front/rear doors).
3. **Check for Locks:** Look for deadbolts, rim locks, keyways, and feel door resistance with the halligan adz.

#### Phase 2: Inward-Swinging Door (Gap, Set, Force) (20 Minutes)
1. **GAP:**
   * Place the bevel of the Halligan **fork** (or adz) against the door edge, 6 inches above or below the lock cylinder.
   * Push the tool into the door stop to create an initial purchase gap between the door and the jamb.
2. **SET:**
   * Halligan firefighter works the fork into the gap, wrapping the curve around the door edge.
   * Halligan firefighter directs the striking firefighter with clear, single-word commands:
     * *"HIT!"* = One crisp, controlled strike on the adz/strike plate.
     * *"HIT!"* = Second strike driving the fork deeper.
     * *"STOP!"* = Cease striking immediately.
   * The fork is "set" when the arch of the fork straddles the inner door jamb.
3. **FORCE:**
   * Halligan firefighter pushes the Halligan bar hard toward the door face (using maximum mechanical leverage).
   * The lock bolt pops past the strike plate, and the door swings inward.
   * **Door Control:** Immediately slip a boot or the axe handle against the door to keep it from swinging wide open and feeding fresh oxygen to an interior fire!

#### Phase 3: Outward-Swinging Door (15 Minutes)
1. Outward swinging doors are the easiest and fastest to force.
2. Insert the **adz end** of the Halligan between the door and the frame near the lock.
3. Command: *"HIT!"* to drive the adz deep behind the door stop.
4. Push the shaft of the Halligan firmly outward (away from the building). The door pops open toward you instantly.

---

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid
- *Curling fingers over the Halligan adz:* When the axe strikes, a missed blow or vibration will crush fingers. Keep hands strictly on the center knurling of the Halligan bar.
- *Swinging the axe like a sledgehammer:* Huge, wide swings miss the target and risk hitting your partner. Short, powerful, 12-to-18-inch strikes from the shoulder offer 10x more control.
- *Losing door control after the pop:* If you pop the door and let it blow open, modern fire dynamics show flow path acceleration can cause a flashover within 60–90 seconds! Chock or control the door.

---

### Tailgate Debrief & Truck Reset Checklist
1. **Tailgate Questions:** *"Were the 'HIT' and 'STOP' verbal commands crisp and loud?"*, *"Did the Halligan firefighter keep their hands out of the strike path?"*
2. **Reset Checklist:**
   - [ ] Axe blade inspected for chips or burrs; file smooth if needed.
   - [ ] Halligan fork and adz checked for bends or cracks.
   - [ ] Married together using standard strap or friction fit and returned to apparatus bracket.
   - [ ] Attendance roster signed.
