# DRILL 04: Ground Ladders – Carries, Raises & Placement
**Category:** Ground Ladders | **Standard:** NFPA 1001 Level 1 (JPR 4.3.6, 4.3.12) / WorkSafeNB Reg 91-191  
**Est. Time:** 45–60 min | **Apparatus:** Pumper / Ladder Rack | **PPE:** Full Structural Turnout Gear & Gloves  
**Roles:** Drill Leader (Facilitator) | Safety Officer (Watcher) | 2-Firefighter Teams

---

### Objective & Rural Fireground Value
Every firefighter must be able to remove a 24-ft or 28-ft extension ladder from the apparatus, carry it safely using 1-person and 2-person methods, raise and extend it to a target window or roofline, lock the dogs, tie off the halyard, and set the proper 75-degree climbing angle. Ground ladders are the primary secondary egress for interior crews operating in two-story rural dwellings.

---

### Equipment & Station Setup
- Apparatus ground ladder complement: 24-ft or 28-ft two-section extension ladder, 14-ft roof ladder.
- Station exterior wall, drill tower, or suitable clear wall structure without overhead wires.
- Ladder staging area clear of potholes, gravel, or ice.

---

### Safety Officer "Watch Outs" & The STOP Rule
- **Overhead Electrical Wires:** Always look up **360 degrees** before removing or raising any ground ladder. Maintain minimum 3-metre (10-foot) clearance from power lines.
- **Pinch Points & Dog Locks:** Keep fingers wrapped around ladder beams—never grasp rungs while raising or lowering the fly section.
- **Footing & Heel Placement:** The "heeler" must place their steel-toed boots against the ladder spurs and pull back on the beams while the extension is raised.
- **The STOP Rule:** Shout **"STOP!"** immediately if the ladder begins to twist, if footing slips, or if anyone touches an overhead hazard.

---

### Step-by-Step Drill Progression

#### Phase 1: Removal, Inspection & Carries (15 Minutes)
1. **Apparatus Removal:** Release ladder retaining bracket. 2 firefighters lift ladder and slide it clear of apparatus bed.
2. **Two-Firefighter Low-Shoulder Carry:**
   * Firefighter at the butt (heel) faces direction of travel. Firefighter at the tip faces direction of travel.
   * Both place upper arm between rungs, resting the beam on the shoulder.
   * Butt firefighter calls: *"Ready to lift... Lift! Ready to walk... Forward!"*
3. **One-Firefighter Low-Shoulder Carry (14-ft roof or 24-ft flat carry):**
   * Center on ladder balance point, reach through rungs, lift onto shoulder, stabilize tip with leading hand.

#### Phase 2: The Two-Firefighter Extension Ladder Raise (20 Minutes)
1. **Positioning:**
   * Butt firefighter places ladder heel on ground at proper distance from wall (~6 feet for a 24' ladder raised to second story).
   * Butt firefighter stands on the bottom rung or places both boots against the spurs, grasping beams firmly at chest level.
2. **Beam Raise (or Flat Raise):**
   * Tip firefighter lifts ladder above head and walks hand-over-hand along the rungs/beams toward the butt, pushing ladder to vertical.
3. **Extension & Dog Lock:**
   * Butt firefighter stabilizes vertical ladder, eyes looking up.
   * Tip firefighter steps to halyard side, grasps rope hand-over-hand, and pulls smoothly to extend fly section.
   * Ensure fly is oriented according to manufacturer specification (usually **fly out** for modern aluminum extension ladders).
   * Once tip reaches 3–5 rungs above roofline (or sill level for rescue), lower slightly until **ladder dogs (pawls) click and lock onto rungs**.
4. **Halyard Tie-Off:**
   * Wrap halyard around the rung below the dogs and tie off with a **clove hitch** and half hitch around the beam.
5. **Setting Angle (1:4 Ratio / 75 Degrees):**
   * Butt firefighter stands straight with toes touching ladder spurs, reaches arms straight forward. Palms should comfortably rest on the ladder rung at shoulder height.

#### Phase 3: Ladder Placement Scenarios (15 Minutes)
Rotate crews through 3 tactical placement positions:
1. **Window Rescue:** Tip placed directly at or just below the windowsill for rapid victim removal.
2. **Ventilation / Entry:** Tip placed upwind side of window, level with upper third of window frame.
3. **Roof Access:** Tip extended minimum **3 to 5 rungs above roof eaves** to provide visible handhold for roof crews in dark conditions.

---

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid
- *Looking down while extending:* The heeler must keep their eyes on the tip to watch for wires and ensure dogs lock properly.
- *Fingers on rungs during lowering:* If halyard slips, fly drops instantly—shearing off fingers! Keep hands strictly on the beams.
- *Shallow climbing angle (<70°):* Causes ladder base to kick out under load. Always verify the arm-reach angle check.

---

### Tailgate Debrief & Truck Reset Checklist
1. **Tailgate Questions:** *"Did anyone forget the halyard knot?"*, *"Was the climbing angle verified before stepping onto the bottom rung?"*
2. **Reset Checklist:**
   - [ ] Fly section lowered smoothly; dogs inspected for spring tension.
   - [ ] Halyard neatly coiled and tied to bottom rungs.
   - [ ] Ladder beams wiped clean of grit and road salt.
   - [ ] Ladder stowed into apparatus rack and locked with safety pins.
   - [ ] Attendance roster signed.
