# The Officer-Less Training Playbook
## How Any Firefighter Can Lead a Safe, High-Impact Weekly Drill

> **"If the bay doors are open and members are here, training happens."**
> In a rural volunteer fire department in New Brunswick, officers are frequently tied up with shift work, family commitments, or out-of-town calls. Training should never be cancelled because an officer isn't present. This playbook gives any member—from a 20-year veteran to a 6-month recruit—the exact framework to step up, facilitate a drill safely, and ensure everyone goes home better prepared.

---

### 1. The Mindset: Facilitator, Not Lecturer
You do **not** need to be a certified fire instructor (NFPA 1041) or a chief officer to run these drills. 
* Your job is **not** to give a 45-minute lecture.
* Your job is to **facilitate hands-on practice**: hold the sheet, read the objectives, stage the gear, keep time, and guide the crew through practical repetitions.
* Tap into the collective experience in the room. If someone has done the skill 100 times, pair them with someone who has done it 5 times.

---

### 2. The Golden Safety Rule: Appoint a Drill Safety Officer
Whenever an officer is not present, **safety is collective, but observation must be dedicated.**

Before touching any apparatus or tool, the group must assign two roles:
1. **Drill Leader (Facilitator):** Keeps the drill moving, reads steps from the 1-page skill sheet, manages time.
2. **Drill Safety Officer (The Watcher):** 
   - **Crucial Rule:** The Safety Officer does **not** participate in the physical evolutions while on watch.
   - Their sole job is watching for:
     - Missing or improper PPE (gloves off, chin straps unfastened, eye protection missing).
     - Trip and slip hazards (water ice in winter, charged hose snags, uneven ground).
     - Pinch points, heavy lifting mechanics, and hydraulic tool path hazards.
     - Fatigue, overheating, or hypothermia.
   - **Emergency Stop Authority:** Anyone can call **"STOP!"** or **"FREEZE!"**, but the Safety Officer has absolute authority to halt any evolution immediately. When "STOP" is called, everyone drops tools, cuts throttle, and stands in place until cleared.

---

### 3. WorkSafeNB & Safety Compliance (General Regulation 91-191)
All training evolutions must adhere to New Brunswick Occupational Health and Safety mandates:
* **The Buddy System (Section 51.6(2)):** No member ever operates in an IDLH (Immediately Dangerous to Life or Health) or simulated IDLH environment alone. Partners must be similarly equipped with matching SCBA air capacity.
* **PPE Standards (Part VII.1):** Full structural PPE (NFPA 1971 coat, trousers, boots, hood, helmet with chinstrap deployed, structural gloves) must be worn for all live handling drills unless specifically noted (e.g., extrication gloves for rescue tool drills).
* **No Jewelry:** As required by NB Reg 91-191, all rings, watches, and loose neck jewelry must be removed before donning gear.
* **Cold Weather Precaution:** Maritime winters mean wet ground turns to black ice in minutes when flowing water. Always have salt/sand ready, and keep pumps in gear or circulating water to prevent freezing discharge valves.

---

### 4. Standard 60-Minute Drill Progression
Every one-page skill sheet in this book follows the proven **3-Phase Evolution**:

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 00:00 - 00:05 | Tailgate Briefing & Role Assignment                          |
|               | - Read drill objective & safety warnings                    |
|               | - Assign Drill Leader & Safety Officer                       |
|               | - Verify PPE & sign attendance roster                        |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 00:05 - 00:15 | Phase 1: Tool Staging & Slow Walkthrough                     |
|               | - Pull equipment from apparatus; inspect readiness           |
|               | - Walk through each step at half speed without timer        |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 00:15 - 00:45 | Phase 2: Crew Repetitions & Skill Building                   |
|               | - Rotate pairs through the evolution                         |
|               | - Experienced members coach newer members                    |
|               | - Add minor realistic conditions (fogged masks, gloves on)  |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 00:45 - 00:55 | Phase 3: Realistic Scenario Rep (Optional Challenge)         |
|               | - Run one complete, timed, or integrated evolution          |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 00:55 - 01:05 | 5-Minute Tailgate Debrief & Truck Reset Checklist            |
|               | - Discuss what worked and what surprised the crew           |
|               | - Reset all apparatus to 100% response readiness             |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

---

### 5. The 5-Minute Tailgate Debrief
Never dismiss the crew immediately after putting tools down. Gather at the tailboard of the primary engine and ask three simple questions:
1. **"What went smooth and fast tonight?"** (Reinforces positive habits)
2. **"What snagged, tripped us up, or caused friction?"** (Identifies gear issues, communication breakdowns, or bad technique)
3. **"If we had to do this at 2:00 AM on a snow-covered dirt road with 3 people, what would bite us?"** (Bridges the gap between parking lot drills and rural fireground reality)

---

### 6. Apparatus Reset Checklist (Never Leave the Hall Unready)
Before anyone heads into the kitchen or goes home, the Drill Leader must confirm:
- [ ] **SCBA:** All cylinders swapped and refilled to full working pressure (4,000–4,500 PSI). Regulators and facepieces cleaned and bagged.
- [ ] **Hose:** Drained completely, wiped, re-packed to department standard (Minuteman / Crosslay / Triple-Layer), nozzles set to proper GPM/fog pattern and bail closed.
- [ ] **Pump:** Water tank topped off 100%. Pump drained and winterized if freezing temperatures are forecast.
- [ ] **Tools & Equipment:** Fuel topped up on saws/portable pumps/power units. Hand tools wiped clean of dirt and oil, stowed in exact bracket.
- [ ] **Battery Tools & Radios:** Handheld radios returned to chargers; rescue tool batteries clicked into charging banks.
- [ ] **Documentation:** Attendance roster filled out and signed for provincial training credit and Fire Marshal records.
