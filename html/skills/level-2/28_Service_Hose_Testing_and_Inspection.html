DRILL 28
Apparatus & Equipment Maintenance
NFPA 1001 Level 2 (JPR 5.5.1) / NFPA 1962

Annual Service Hose Testing & Inspection

⏱️ Time: 60–75 min 🚒 Apparatus: Pumper / Hose Testing Rig 🦺 PPE: Helmet, Safety Glasses, Boots & Gloves 👥 Roles: Drill Leader (Facilitator) | Safety Officer (Watcher) | Testing & Inspection Crew

🎯 Objective & Rural Fireground Value

Every firefighter must know how to inspect fire hose for cuts, chemical degradation, coupling damage, and missing gaskets, connect hose to a test manifold or pumper discharge with a safety test gate valve, bleed all air from the lines, pressurize the layout to NFPA 1962 service test pressure, hold the pressure for 5 minutes, and safely mark or tag out defective sections. A burst hose line inside a burning building is catastrophic.

📦 Tools & Apparatus Setup
  • Pumper or specialized electric hose tester with test gate valve (valve with 1/4" bleeder hole to prevent pressure surges if a hose bursts).
  • Sections of 1.75" (45mm) attack line and 4"/5" (100mm/125mm) LDH supply line.
  • Test nozzles or threaded test caps with petcock bleeder valves.
  • Spanner wrenches, grease pencil/paint marker, hose record log sheets.
  • 100-ft tape measure.
⚠️ Safety Watch-Outs & STOP Rule
  • Catastrophic Burst Hazard: During the 5-minute pressurized hold, NO PERSONNEL MAY STRADDLE OR WALK ALONG THE HOSE LINES! Stand a minimum of 15 feet to the side (the "safe zone").
  • Trapped Air Hazard: Air compresses under pressure; water does not. Trapped air turns a bursting hose into an explosive whip. ALL AIR MUST BE COMPLETELY BLED before raising pressure!
🛑 THE STOP RULE: Shout "STOP!" immediately if anyone approaches a pressurized line, or if a coupling begins to slip noticeably from the hose collar.
Phase 1: Visual Inspection & Coupling Check (15 Minutes) Slow Dry Run
  • Lay hose straight on flat pavement.
  • Jacket Inspection: Check for mold/mildew rot, cuts, heavy abrasion, burns, or chemical stains (battery acid, motor oil).
  • Coupling & Gasket Inspection:
    • Swivel must rotate smoothly by hand without binding.
    • Inspect female swivel gasket: Must be supple, free of cracks, and seated flat in the groove.
    • Check lugs and threads for dents, burrs, or flattening.
    • Check Storz locks for spring tension and intact locking levers.
  • Marking the Coupling Seam: Draw a line with a grease pencil around the hose jacket directly where it meets the metal coupling ferrule. (If the coupling slips during pressure testing, this pencil line will move, revealing a failing connection!).

Apparatus & Equipment Maintenance • Practical Application
Phase 2: Laying Out & Bleeding Air (20 Minutes) Buddy Coaching
  • Layout Rules:
    • Maximum length of any single test line connected to a discharge is 300 feet (90m).
    • Keep lines straight without sharp kinks or bends.
  • Connecting & Bleeding:
    • Connect hose to pumper test gate valve or hose tester manifold.
    • Connect test cap or shutoff nozzle to the far end.
    • Open the test gate valve and charge the layout with low hydrant/tank pressure (~45–50 PSI).
    • Elevate the far end test cap/nozzle slightly and open the bleeder petcock until all trapped air escapes in a steady stream of solid water.
    • Close the bleeder valve and verify the test line is completely full of water with zero air pockets.
Phase 3: Pressurization, 5-Minute Hold & Log Sign-Off (20 Minutes) Timed / Stress
  • Service Test Pressures (NFPA 1962 Standard):
    • Attack Hose (1.75" / 2.5"): Typically tested at 250 to 300 PSI (or original stenciled service test pressure).
    • Supply Line / LDH (4" / 5"): Typically tested at 200 PSI.
  • Ramping Pressure:
    • Raise pump pressure slowly at a rate not exceeding 25 PSI per second.
    • Once target service test pressure is reached, close the test gate valve.
    • Start the timer: Hold pressure for 5 MINUTES.
    • Personnel monitor lines from the designated safe zone 15 feet away.
  • Post-Test Inspection:
    • After 5 minutes, bleed pressure down slowly to zero.
    • Inspect coupling pencil marks: If the line moved more than 1/8 inch (3mm) away from the coupling, or if water weeping occurred around the shank, the hose FAILS.
    • If any leaks, pinholes, or coupling slippage occur: Cut the defective hose coupling off or spray-paint a red "X" and tag out of service.
    • Record hose ID numbers, test date, test pressure, and pass/fail status in the department NFPA log.

    ---

⚠️ Common Pitfalls & Fireground Hazards
  • Testing hose with trapped air: Air acts as a loaded spring. A burst hose with trapped air will explode and throw shrapnel; a hose with 100% water simply splits with a harmless splash.
  • Walking over charged test lines: High-pressure testing exposes latent weaknesses in fabric. Never step over or straddle lines under test!
  • Ramping pressure too fast: Spiking pressure instantly damages internal rubber liners and blows out pump packing.
🗣️ 5-Minute Tailgate Debrief
  1. "Did any pencil marks move on our older hose couplings?", "Why must hose be tested annually under NFPA standards?"
✅ Apparatus Reset Checklist
  • All passed hose sections stamped/logged with test year and stowed back on apparatus beds.
  • Any failed hose tagged, marked with red tape, and placed in repair bin.
  • Test caps and manifold dried and returned to maintenance cabinet.
  • Hose test log signed and filed for Fire Marshal / FUS records.
  • Attendance roster signed.
