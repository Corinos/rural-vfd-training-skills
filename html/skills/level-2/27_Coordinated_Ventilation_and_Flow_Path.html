NFPA 1001 Level 2 (JPR 5.3.1, 5.3.2) / UL FSRI Research

Coordinated Fireground Ventilation & Flow Path Control

⏱️ Time: 45–60 min 🚒 Apparatus: Pumper / Engine 🦺 PPE: Full Structural Turnout Gear & SCBA 👥 Roles: Drill Leader (Facilitator) | Safety Officer (Watcher) | Interior Attack Team & Exterior Vent Team

🎯 Objective & Rural Fireground Value

Every firefighter must understand modern fire dynamics (UL/NIST research): how opening doors and windows creates a Flow Path feeding fresh oxygen to ventilation-controlled fires, how uncontrolled ventilation triggers rapid flashovers, and how to execute Coordinated Ventilation synchronized with water application (including exterior transitional attack).

📦 Tools & Apparatus Setup
  • Apparatus pumper with charged 1.75" (45mm) attack line (smooth bore or combination nozzle).
  • Ventilation tools: Halligan bar, flathead axe, 6-ft pike pole.
  • Station bay doors, window prop, or simulated structure layout.
  • Smoke machine or cardboard flow-path demo box (dollhouse prop).
⚠️ Safety Watch-Outs & STOP Rule
  • The Flashover Clock: In modern homes filled with petroleum-based synthetic furnishings (plastics, poly foam), opening a door or breaking a window to a ventilation-limited fire accelerates flashover in as little as 90 to 120 seconds.
  • Wind-Driven Hazards: Never vent on the upwind side if crews are operating inside! Wind forces superheated gases directly onto interior firefighters.
🛑 THE STOP RULE: Shout "STOP!" immediately if an exterior window or door is opened before the interior nozzle crew is in position with a charged, flowing line.
Phase 1: Understanding the Flow Path & Modern Fire Behavior (15 Minutes) Slow Dry Run
  • Legacy vs Modern Fires:
    • Legacy homes (natural wood/cotton): Fire was fuel-limited; took 20+ minutes to flashover.
    • Modern homes (plastics/synthetics): Rapidly consume available oxygen and become ventilation-limited (oxygen-starved) while producing massive volumes of unburned fuel gases.
  • The Flow Path Definition:
    • The space between an air intake (open door/window) and an exhaust opening (chimney, roof hole, or upper window).
    • Bidirectional Flow Path: Fresh oxygen rushes in low; black superheated smoke pushes out high (neutral plane).
    • Unidirectional Flow Path: Opening where air is strictly entering (intake) or strictly exhausting under high pressure (blowtorch exhaust).

DRILL 27: Practical Evolutions & Tailgate Debrief

Advanced Tactics • Practical Application
Phase 2: Door Control & Flow Path Isolation (15 Minutes) Buddy Coaching
  • The Entry Door: The front door is a massive oxygen intake.
  • Door Control Drill:
    • Forcible entry team pops the door.
    • Immediate Action: Pull the door almost completely shut again, leaving only a 2-inch crack while awaiting the charged hoseline.
    • Chock or hold the door closed to starve the fire of fresh air while the nozzle team flakes hose and dons facepieces.
    • When attack team is on air with line charged: open door, enter low, and control the door behind the hose to minimize incoming air.
Phase 3: Synchronized Coordinated Attack (Transitional Attack) (20 Minutes) Timed / Stress
  • The Exterior "Transitional" Knockdown:
    • Common in rural NB when arriving with 2–3 firefighters who cannot legally enter IDLH under WorkSafeNB 2-in/2-out rules.
    • Position at an exterior window showing heavy fire.
    • Direct a solid or straight stream steeply upward against the ceiling of the fire room.
    • Flow for 15 to 30 seconds without moving the stream around.
    • Water coats the ceiling, turns to steam, absorbs heat, and dramatically knocks down the fire without pushing heat into adjacent rooms.
  • Coordinating the Interior Advance with Ventilation:
    • Exterior vent team stands by at the designated exhaust window with Halligan/pike pole.
    • DO NOT BREAK THE WINDOW YET.
    • Interior nozzle team enters, advances to the fire compartment, and begins cooling the ceiling gases with water.
    • Interior Team Leader calls over radio: "Interior to Vent, we have water on the fire! Take the Side Bravo window!"
    • Vent team clears the window completely (glass, sash, and curtains) to establish clean exhaust.

    ---

⚠️ Common Pitfalls & Fireground Hazards
  • Ventilating before water is ready: Breaking windows while the engine is still laying hose feeds fresh oxygen to the fire, causing rapid structural flashover.
  • Failing to clear the window sash: Leaving window sashes or heavy blinds in place reduces ventilation efficiency by over 50%. Clean the entire window opening from top to bottom!
  • Whipping the nozzle in circles during transitional attack: Rapidly swirling the nozzle creates high air entrainment, acting like a giant fan that pushes heat throughout the house. Hold the stream still against the ceiling!
🗣️ 5-Minute Tailgate Debrief
  1. "What happens to an oxygen-starved fire when we open the front door?", "Why must the vent team wait for the radio call from the nozzle team before breaking glass?"
✅ Apparatus Reset Checklist
  • Pike poles and Halligan bars inspected and remounted.
  • Broken glass or prop debris swept and disposed of.
  • Hose drained, rolled, and repacked.
  • Attendance roster signed.
