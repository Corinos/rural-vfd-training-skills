DRILL 26
Advanced Fire Suppression
NFPA 1001 Level 2 (JPR 5.3.3) / NFPA 58

Flammable Gas / Propane Tank Cooling & Valve Shutoff

⏱️ Time: 45–60 min 🚒 Apparatus: Pumper / Engine 🦺 PPE: Full Structural Turnout Gear, SCBA (On Air) & Flash Hood 👥 Roles: Drill Leader (Facilitator) | Safety Officer (Watcher) | 2 Handline Attack Teams (4–6 Firefighters)

🎯 Objective & Rural Fireground Value

Every firefighter must know how to safely manage a pressurized LPG / Propane Tank fire or leak, recognize signs of an impending BLEVE (Boiling Liquid Expanding Vapor Explosion), advance toward the tank using dual interlocking wide-fog streams for thermal shielding, cool the vapor space of the cylinder, and execute a manual shutoff of the main control valve. Propane tanks (100-lb cylinders, 500-to-1,000 gallon domestic "pigs") are universal heating fuel sources across rural New Brunswick.

📦 Tools & Apparatus Setup
  • Pumper supplying two 1.75" (45mm) attack lines at 100 PSI nozzle pressure.
  • 2 combination fog nozzles set to wide-fog shield pattern (minimum 30–60 degrees).
  • Training propane tank prop (20-lb BBQ tank, 100-lb cylinder, or scrap 500-gallon tank simulator).
  • Water supply established (minimum 1,500+ gallons on site before starting live water evolutions).
⚠️ Safety Watch-Outs & STOP Rule
  • THE BLEVE DANGER: If flame impinges directly on the vapor space (top 20–50% of the tank) without cooling water, the steel weakens and can violently rupture within 8 to 15 minutes.
  • BLEVE Warning Signs: A rising pitch in the sound of roaring gas venting from the relief valve, or visible discoloration/bulging of the tank shell. EVACUATE IMMEDIATELY (Minimum 500 metres / 1,500 feet)!
  • Continuous Water Supply Mandatory: Never approach a burning pressurized vessel unless an uninterrupted, positive water supply is guaranteed.
🛑 THE STOP RULE: Shout "STOP!" immediately if either hose line loses water pressure, if nozzles fall out of interlock, or if a firefighter trips during the approach.
Phase 1: Propane Properties & Approach Strategy (15 Minutes) Slow Dry Run
  • Properties of Propane (LPG):
    • Vapor density is 1.5 (heavier than air); leaking gas pools in low ditches, basements, and around foundations.
    • Expansion ratio: 270 to 1 (1 liter of liquid expands into 270 liters of explosive gas!).
  • The Dual-Line Interlocking Shield:
    • Two 1.75" lines advance side-by-side.
    • Both nozzles set to wide-fog pattern (45–60 degrees).
    • The two fog streams overlap by 1 to 2 feet in the center, creating a solid, impenetrable thermal water wall protecting the firefighters behind them.
    • The fog stream pushes radiant heat and flames away while entraining fresh air for the crew.

DRILL 26: Practical Evolutions & Tailgate Debrief

Advanced Fire Suppression • Practical Application
Phase 2: Coordinated Approach Walkthrough (15 Minutes) Buddy Coaching
  • Both nozzle teams kneel or crouch side-by-side, shoulder-to-shoulder.
  • Team Leader (between the two nozzlemen) coordinates cadence: "Step... Step... Step..."
  • Move forward at a slow, deliberate pace. Nozzlemen keep streams focused on the upper shell (vapor space) of the propane tank to keep the steel cold.
  • If the relief valve is burning, DO NOT extinguish the flame until the valve can be shut! Extinguishing the flame before shutting the leak creates an invisible cloud of heavier-than-air gas that will ignite into a catastrophic vapor cloud explosion.
Phase 3: Valve Isolation & Safe Retreat (20 Minutes) Timed / Stress
  • As the crew reaches arm's length from the tank:
    • Both nozzlemen open their fog patterns wide to deflect flames around the sides.
    • The Team Leader (or backup firefighter) reaches between the streams with gloved hands.
    • Grasp the handwheel of the service valve and rotate clockwise until fully closed.
  • Confirm Extinguishment:
    • Once the valve is closed, the flame will starve and extinguish.
    • Continue cooling the hot tank shell with fog streams until steam generation stops.
  • The Coordinated Retreat:
    • NEVER TURN YOUR BACK ON THE TANK!
    • Keeping both fog streams flowing toward the tank, the crew backs up step-by-step in unison until clear of the hot zone (minimum 50 feet away).
    • Nozzles are shut down slowly to prevent water hammer.

    ---

⚠️ Common Pitfalls & Fireground Hazards
  • Turning off nozzles when the fire goes out: If the valve didn't seal 100%, gas continues leaking; shutting off the water shield leaves you unprotected against re-ignition. Keep the shield up while backing out!
  • Splitting the fog patterns: If nozzlemen pull apart, a gap opens in the center of the water shield, allowing superheated flames to blast between them. Keep streams firmly interlocked.
  • Approaching from the ends of the tank: If a cylinder ruptures, the convex end caps shoot outward like missile rockets. Always approach from the sides (perpendicular to the tank ends)!
🗣️ 5-Minute Tailgate Debrief
  1. "How loud was the water shield roaring during the approach?", "Why must we approach the sides of the tank rather than the ends?"
✅ Apparatus Reset Checklist
  • Hoses drained, dried, and repacked.
  • Nozzles flushed and set to department default pattern.
  • SCBA facepieces disinfected; cylinders refilled.
  • Attendance roster signed.
