DRILL 11
Rural Water Supply
NFPA 1001 Level 1 (JPR 4.3.15) / NFPA 1002

Drafting from a Static Water Source & Dry Hydrant

⏱️ Time: 60–75 min 🚒 Apparatus: Pumper / Engine 🦺 PPE: Full Gear or Work Boots/Gloves (PFD if near water edge) 👥 Roles: Drill Leader (Facilitator) | Safety Officer (Watcher) | Pump Operator & Suction Crew

🎯 Objective & Rural Fireground Value

Every firefighter must be able to assemble 10 to 20 feet of hard suction hose with a strainer, connect to the pumper's master intake, submerge the strainer into a static water source (pond, river, stream, cistern) or couple to a municipal dry hydrant, pull a continuous prime within 30 seconds, and transition from tank water to draft without cavitation. The foundation of rural firefighting.

📦 Tools & Apparatus Setup
  • Pumper with 2 sections of 6-inch (150mm) hard suction hose and master intake.
  • Barrel strainer (with tie-off rope) and floating dock/strainer or dry hydrant adapter.
  • Static water source: Station cistern, dry hydrant, local pond, brook, or drafting pit.
  • Personal Flotation Devices (PFDs) for any members working within 3 metres (10 feet) of open water.
⚠️ Safety Watch-Outs & STOP Rule
  • Water Edge Safety: Any member working within 10 feet of a steep riverbank, pond edge, or ice shelf must wear a PFD (life jacket). Never wear full structural turnout gear onto untested winter ice.
  • Vacuum Gauge Monitoring: Monitor compound gauge during priming. If vacuum exceeds 22 inches of mercury (inHg) or fluctuates wildly, cavitation is occurring.
🛑 THE STOP RULE: Shout "STOP!" immediately if bank soil collapses near the water edge, or if a hard suction hose slips off a coupling.
Phase 1: Hard Suction Coupling & Strainer Selection (15 Minutes) Slow Dry Run
  • Coupling Hard Suction:
    • Two firefighters grasp the 10-foot hard suction section.
    • Inspect the thick rubber gasket in the female swivel coupling for dirt or tears.
    • Thread coupling onto the pumper's master intake and torque tight with rubber mallet or spanner wrenches (an air leak as small as a pinhole will prevent pulling a prime!).
  • Selecting & Rigging the Strainer:
    • Barrel Strainer: For deep water (>4 feet). Requires minimum 2 feet of water above the strainer to prevent surface whirlpools, and 1 foot of clearance from muddy bottom. Tie a rope around the strainer eyelet to control depth.
    • Floating Strainer: For shallow streams or ponds (12 to 24 inches deep). Floats on surface and drafts from bottom.
    • Dry Hydrant Connection: Couple hard suction directly to the 4.5" or 6" dry hydrant threaded/Storz head.

DRILL 11: Practical Evolutions & Tailgate Debrief

Rural Water Supply • Practical Application
Phase 2: Pulling the Prime (20 Minutes) Buddy Coaching
  • Pre-Priming Pump Setup:
    • Shift transmission into PUMP GEAR (confirm speedometer reads ~15–20 mph or green "Pump Engaged" light illuminates).
    • Confirm Tank-to-Pump valve is CLOSED (vital: an open tank valve allows tank water to satisfy the pump, preventing vacuum from reaching the suction hose).
    • Confirm all discharge valves, bleeders, and drain valves are sealed 100% airtight.
  • Activating the Primer:
    • Set engine throttle to recommended priming RPM (typically 1,000–1,200 RPM).
    • Pull and hold the Primer Control Valve.
    • Observe the Compound (Intake) Gauge: Needle should swing counter-clockwise into vacuum, pulling 10 to 20 inches of mercury (inHg).
    • Listen for tone of primer to change from high-pitched whine to deep rumble as water hits the primer vanes.
    • Watch for a steady stream of water discharging from the primer exhaust tube beneath the truck.
    • Once prime is established (steady discharge), release the primer valve smoothly.
    • Master pressure gauge will immediately climb to 50–100 PSI.
Phase 3: Transition & Maintaining Continuous Flow (20 Minutes) Timed / Stress
  • Open a discharge line (or deck gun / crosslay directed back into the water source) slowly.
  • Adjust engine throttle to maintain desired discharge pressure (100–120 PSI).
  • Open the Pump Cooler / Recirculating Line slightly to keep pump from overheating if no water is flowing through nozzles.
  • Practice identifying Cavitation: Gravelly rattle in the pump casing and needle bouncing on the discharge gauge. Fix immediately by throttling down or reducing flow.

    ---

⚠️ Common Pitfalls & Fireground Hazards
  • Leaving a bleeder valve cracked open: Even a 1/8-inch drain valve left open admits enough atmospheric air to destroy the pump's vacuum!
  • Resting strainer in silt/mud: Sucking mud, leaves, or stones into the pump clogs impellers and burns out shaft seals. Always suspend the strainer using a tie-off rope.
  • Forgetting winter ice buildup: In NB winters, dry hydrants can freeze internally from ground frost. Always check dry hydrant barrel with a weighted plumb line before connecting.
🗣️ 5-Minute Tailgate Debrief
  1. "How many seconds did it take to achieve prime?", "Did we hear any air hissing around the hard suction coupling gaskets?"
✅ Apparatus Reset Checklist
  • Pump drained: Open master pump drain to clear all raw pond water.
  • Flush pump with clean water from onboard booster tank if brackish/muddy water was drafted.
  • Hard suction disconnected, drained, and secured back in brackets.
  • Strainer cleaned of weeds/debris.
  • Attendance roster signed.
