DRILL 17
Salvage & Overhaul
NFPA 1001 Level 1 (JPR 4.3.13, 4.3.14) / WorkSafeNB Reg 91-191

Salvage, Water Chutes & Overhaul with TIC

⏱️ Time: 45–60 min 🚒 Apparatus: Pumper / Rescue 🦺 PPE: Full Structural Turnout Gear & Gloves 👥 Roles: Drill Leader (Facilitator) | Safety Officer (Watcher) | Salvage Crew Pairs

🎯 Objective & Rural Fireground Value

Every firefighter must be able to deploy a heavy salvage cover (tarp) using the Two-Firefighter Balloon Throw, construct a functional Water Chute and Catch-All Basin to divert ceiling runoff out of a building, and utilize a Thermal Imaging Camera (TIC) alongside pike poles to locate concealed fire in ceilings and wall cavities without causing unnecessary structural destruction.

📦 Tools & Apparatus Setup
  • 2 heavy-duty canvas or vinyl salvage covers (minimum 12' x 14' or 14' x 18').
  • 2 pike poles (6-ft fiberglass or wood).
  • 1 Thermal Imaging Camera (TIC).
  • Simulated furniture pile or tables stacked together.
  • Clear water bucket or garden hose for water diversion demonstration.
⚠️ Safety Watch-Outs & STOP Rule
  • Overhead Collapse: During overhaul and ceiling pull, never stand directly underneath the drywall/plaster being pulled. Pull from an angle with your back to an exit!
  • Eye & Respiratory Protection: Wear eye protection and keep SCBA on air until the atmosphere has been metered and cleared of toxic particulate and carbon monoxide.
🛑 THE STOP RULE: Shout "STOP!" immediately if ceiling joists show signs of structural sag or if heavy water buildup threatens catastrophic ceiling collapse.
Phase 1: Furniture Grouping & The Balloon Throw (15 Minutes) Slow Dry Run
  • Grouping Contents:
    • Move household furniture (dressers, chairs, electronics) to the center of the room, away from exterior walls and light fixtures.
    • Place delicate electronics and photos on top of beds or tables; elevate furniture off wet carpet using blocks.
  • The Two-Firefighter Balloon Throw:
    • Two firefighters position on opposite sides of the grouped pile, holding the folded salvage cover.
    • Unfold the cover lengthwise between them.
    • Each firefighter grasps the outer corners with palms facing down.
    • On count: "1, 2, 3, LIFT!" Both firefighters make a high, sweeping upward motion, capturing a "balloon" of air beneath the tarp.
    • Guide the floating tarp smoothly over the top of the furniture stack and tuck the bottom edges under the furniture legs to shed water.

Salvage & Overhaul • Practical Application
Phase 2: Constructing a Water Chute (15 Minutes) Buddy Coaching

    (Used when water is leaking through an upper floor ceiling and must be diverted out a window or doorway)

  • Lay a salvage cover flat on the floor.
  • Place two 6-foot pike poles along the outer edges of the tarp, parallel to each other.
  • Roll the sides of the tarp tightly inward around the pike poles 3 to 4 turns, creating a heavy-duty trough with 6-inch side walls.
  • Position the elevated end beneath the ceiling drip, propped on a ladder, cabinet, or held by a pike pole prop.
  • Direct the lower downhill end of the trough out of an open window or exterior door.
  • Test by pouring a bucket of water down the chute; confirm 100% water diversion without side spills.
Phase 3: Constructing a Catch-All Basin (10 Minutes) Timed / Stress
  • Lay salvage cover flat on the floor beneath an overhead leak.
  • Fold each edge inward approximately 3 feet.
  • Fold the corners at 45-degree angles to create interlocking side walls.
  • Result: A waterproof basin that can hold 100 to 200 gallons of dirty runoff, protecting hardwood floors and carpets.

    #### Phase 4: Overhaul with the Thermal Imaging Camera (TIC) (15 Minutes)

  • Operating the TIC:
    • Power on TIC; verify battery level and temperature scale mode.
    • Scan technique: Ceiling -> Walls -> Floor in a systematic "Z" sweep.
  • Identifying Concealed Fire:
    • Explain white-hot vs colorized temperature gradients.
    • Identify thermal bridges, heat signatures around electrical outlets, recessed lighting, and heating ducts.
  • Coordinated Ceiling Pull:
    • Firefighter with TIC identifies hot spot in ceiling.
    • Second firefighter positions with 6-ft pike pole with back to exit.
    • Drives pike pole point through drywall with blade parallel to joists.
    • Twists tool 90 degrees across joists and pulls down with body weight, stepping backward to let debris fall safely ahead.

    ---

⚠️ Common Pitfalls & Fireground Hazards
  • Pulling ceiling directly above your head: Falling plaster, sheetrock, and insulation will crush your helmet, obscure your vision, or drop hot embers down your coat collar. Always pull away from you!
  • Using dirty/punctured salvage covers: A torn tarp simply funnels dirty water onto the homeowner's valuables. Inspect and wash covers after every fire.
🗣️ 5-Minute Tailgate Debrief
  1. "Did the balloon throw clear the pile without snagging?", "How much cleaner is overhaul when directed by a TIC rather than blind pulling?"
✅ Apparatus Reset Checklist
  • Salvage covers washed clean of mud and soot, hung to dry, or folded into standard firefighter accordion fold.
  • Pike pole heads cleaned and inspected for cracks.
  • TIC screen wiped with soft cloth; battery clicked into charger.
  • Attendance roster signed.
