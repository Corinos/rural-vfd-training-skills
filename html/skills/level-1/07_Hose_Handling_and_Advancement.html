DRILL 07
Hose & Streams
NFPA 1001 Level 1 (JPR 4.3.10) / WorkSafeNB Reg 91-191

Hose Handling, Body Mechanics & Advancement

⏱️ Time: 45–60 min 🚒 Apparatus: Pumper / Engine 🦺 PPE: Full Structural Turnout Gear & Gloves 👥 Roles: Drill Leader (Facilitator) | Safety Officer (Watcher) | Nozzleman & Backup Firefighter

🎯 Objective & Rural Fireground Value

Every firefighter must master modern hose handling mechanics (the Hip Grip or Arm-Clamp technique) to absorb 60–80 lbs of nozzle reaction force without fatigue, coordinate advancement with a backup firefighter, negotiate 90-degree corners and stairs, and maintain strict door control. Proper technique prevents fatigue and prevents nozzlemen from being thrown off balance.

📦 Tools & Apparatus Setup
  • 1 charged 1.75" (45mm) attack line (100–150 ft) flowing at normal fireground operating pressure (~100–125 PSI discharge pressure, delivering 150–160 GPM / 600 LPM).
  • Simulated corner/hallway (between apparatus bays, around building corners, or training door prop).
  • Webbing strap (for door control).
⚠️ Safety Watch-Outs & STOP Rule
  • Back Strain & Ergonomics: Watch for nozzlemen leaning backward or fighting nozzle reaction with bent arms. The nozzle reaction must be driven straight down into the hips and legs.
  • Pinch Points & Knuckles: Ensure hands are not dragged along brick/door frames when navigating tight corners under pressure.
  • Water Runoff: Direct runoff toward exterior storm drains or open gravel; monitor for slick surfaces in freezing weather.
🛑 THE STOP RULE: Shout "STOP!" if the nozzle firefighter loses grip or control of the bail, or if a member falls.
Phase 1: Modern Nozzle Mechanics (The Hip Grip) (15 Minutes) Slow Dry Run
  • The Flaw of the "Death Grip":
    • Grasping the nozzle with two hands held out in front of the chest burns out shoulder and arm muscles in under 2 minutes.
  • The Modern Hip Grip Technique:
    • Tuck the charged hose line firmly between your hip and upper arm (or lock it under your armpit against your ribcage).
    • Rest your forward hand on the hose 6–8 inches behind the nozzle coupling.
    • Rest your rear hand on top of the nozzle shutoff bail.
    • Lean forward aggressively with your front knee bent and back leg braced (athletic boxer stance).
    • The backward thrust of the water stream is absorbed directly by your core and hips into the ground.

Phase 2: The Backup Firefighter's Role (15 Minutes) Buddy Coaching
  • Positioning:
    • The backup firefighter positions on the same side of the hose as the nozzleman, 3 to 4 feet behind.
    • Lock your forward arm around the hose and rest your back hand firmly against the nozzleman's SCBA cylinder harness or shoulder.
  • Absorbing Reaction & Pushing Forward:
    • The backup absorbs any remaining backward reaction, allowing the nozzleman to direct the stream freely with minimal effort.
    • When moving forward, the backup firefighter provides the forward drive: "Moving forward in 3, 2, 1... Push!"
Phase 3: Corner Navigation & Door Control Advancement (20 Minutes) Timed / Stress
  • Navigating a 90-Degree Corner:
    • Never let the hose pin the nozzleman against the outside wall of a corner.
    • The backup firefighter drops to one knee at the corner vertex, becoming a "human pulley", holding the hose off the wall so slack feeds freely to the nozzleman.
    • Nozzleman advances 10–15 feet into the room, sweeps the ceiling and floor, and calls for more line.
  • Door Control & Chocking:
    • Before entering, firefighter checks the door for heat with gloved hand/TIC.
    • Chock the door open using a wooden wedge or hose strap so the door cannot pinch the charged hose and cut off water!

    ---

⚠️ Common Pitfalls & Fireground Hazards
  • Standing upright while flowing: Standing tall raises your center of gravity; nozzle reaction will push you backwards. Stay low and lean into the stream.
  • Backup firefighter on opposite side: Straddling the hose or standing on opposite sides creates a scissors hazard if the line whips or snags.
  • Letting the door close on the hose: A self-closing spring hinge will pinch a charged 1.75" line against the jamb, choking water flow to a trickle. Always chock the door!
🗣️ 5-Minute Tailgate Debrief
  1. "How big of a difference did the hip grip make in muscle fatigue?", "How well did the backup firefighter communicate forward movement?"
✅ Apparatus Reset Checklist
  • Nozzle closed smoothly to prevent water hammer.
  • Pump throttle brought to idle; discharge gate closed.
  • Hose drained, rolled, and repacked.
  • Door wedges returned to gear pockets.
  • Attendance roster signed.
